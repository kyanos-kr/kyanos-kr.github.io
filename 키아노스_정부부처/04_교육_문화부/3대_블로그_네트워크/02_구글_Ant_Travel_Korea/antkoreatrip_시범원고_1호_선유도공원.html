"In the pulsing heart of Seoul, a forgotten concrete island transformed into a verdant oasis: Seonyudo, where nature whispers poetry amidst the urban symphony."

Seonyudo Island Park: Korea's first recycled eco-park on the Han River / Photo by Python

Nestled in the serene expanse of the Han River, Seonyudo Island Park emerges as a living testament to the transformative power of time. Once a hulking industrial water filtration facility that treated 400,000 tons of tap water daily for the growing metropolis, it now stands as an internationally celebrated sanctuary of rebirth. In this calm pocket between Yeouido and Mapo, thick ivy blankets industrial concrete pillars, weeping willows brush mirror-like pools, and Seoul's relentless pace softens into meditative silence.

Why Visit Seonyudo Island Park?

  • Architectural Rebirth & Decay Aesthetics — Instead of demolishing the decommissioned 1970s filtration basins, landscape architects preserved the rusted steel and stark concrete columns, creating the "Time Garden" where nature and industry coexist in haunting beauty.
  • Unrivaled Sunset Over the Han River — The elegant arched pedestrian bridge (Seonyugyo) offers an unobstructed vantage point of the golden hour setting behind the Seongsan Bridge and the Seoul skyline.
  • Cyclist's Oasis on the Han River Trail — Located directly adjacent to the southern Han River bicycle highway, it serves as a peaceful rest stop for riders exploring Seoul from east to west.

Getting There: Step-by-Step Route

By Seoul Metro (Fastest & Easiest)

  1. Subway Line 9 (Seonyudo Station / 선유도역): Take Exit 2. Walk straight north for about 700 meters (roughly 10 minutes) toward Yanghwa Hangang Park, then cross over the arched pedestrian bridge (Seonyugyo) directly onto the island.
  2. Subway Line 2 or 9 (Dangsan Station / 당산역): Take Exit 13, board local bus #5714, and alight at the "Seonyudo Park" stop right at the center of Yanghwa Bridge.

By Bicycle (The Local Experience)

Ride along the South Han River Bike Path (남단 자전거도로). When passing Yanghwa Hangang Park, dismount at the elevator or ramp leading to Seonyugyo Bridge. Bicycles must be walked across the pedestrian bridge and parked at the designated bike racks near the park entrance to protect pedestrians and resting wildlife.

Insider Tips & Best Photo Spots

  • The Golden Hour at Seonyugyo Bridge: Arrive roughly 45 minutes before official sunset. Standing midway across the French-designed pedestrian arch gives you an epic view of orange reflections dancing across the wide Han River.
  • The Time Garden (시간의 정원): Walk down the wooden stairs between the concrete chambers. In the mornings, soft beams of sunlight pierce through the open roof, creating ethereal volumetric light rays that photographers adore.
  • Local Food Nearby: After your walk, head back toward Dangsan Station Exit 6 or Seonyudo Station Exit 2 for hearty Korean pork belly BBQ (Samgyeopsal, 삼겹살) or comforting warm pollack soup (Dongtae-tang, 동태탕) priced around 10,000–14,000 KRW ($7.50–$10 USD).
  • Park Etiquette: Alcohol consumption, loud music, and camping tents are strictly prohibited on the island. Drones require strict military airspace permits due to central Seoul flight regulations.

A Philosopher's Note

Modern cities are obsessed with building anew, erasing whatever grows brittle or outlives its initial utility. Yet Seonyudo reminds us of the profound grace in allowing old wounds to heal into quiet poetry. What once forced nature to obey human machinery has become a temple where water lilies purify the current without a single whirring motor. Here, amidst the rust and damp concrete, one realizes that true progress is not domination over nature, but reconciliation with it.

Plan Your Visit

  • Combine with: A bicycle ride along Yanghwa Hangang Park or a coffee break in nearby hipster Mullae-dong Art Village (문래창작촌), just two subway stops away.
  • Essential Gear: T-money transportation card, a light windbreaker for the breezy bridge, and a camera with a fast prime lens for low-light garden portraits.
