Nestled deep along the pristine headwaters of the Bukhangang River, the quiet mountain county of Hwacheon transforms each January into a world-renowned frozen playground. Recognised by global media including CNN as one of the “Seven Winter Wonders of the World,” the Hwacheon Sancheoneo Ice Festival welcomes well over a million adventurers every season. Here is our authentic, on-the-scene chronicle capturing the raw excitement, laughter, and heartwarming hospitality of this extraordinary Korean winter festival.

1. Entering the Frozen Festival: A County Transformed

Setting out early from Seoul under crisp morning skies, the open highway gradually gave way to the winding mountain roads of Gangwon Province. By the time noon arrived, we reached the outskirts of Hwacheon-gun. Even on an ordinary weekday, the tranquil county was pulsing with an electric carnival atmosphere that was impossible to miss.

As we entered the town perimeter, roads were packed in bumper-to-bumper harmony reminiscent of a bustling metropolitan hub. Yet what immediately stood out was the orderly grace with which traffic flowed: county civil servants, military personnel, and elderly local volunteers were stationed at every intersection, waving warm greetings and guiding visitors with bright smiles and indefatigable spirit.

▲ Sky High Festival Welcome: Giant festival ad-balloons soaring high against the crystal winter sky / Photo by Paeton

Before you even reach the main entrance gates, you instantly feel that this entire valley belongs to the festival. Colorful banners fluttered in the breeze, vibrant welcoming structures heralded the event, and massive ad-balloons hovered high in the cobalt winter sky. Located on the pristine, unpolluted upper reaches of the Bukhangang River, the staggering physical scale of the frozen grounds leaves first-time visitors in sheer awe.

2. The Thousand-Hole Ice Fishing Miracle: Half People, Half Fish

While the winter sun had gently softened the midday air to make heavy parkas feel almost cumbersome, the frozen riverbed remained rock-solid. A layer of ice more than 30 centimeters thick stretched as far as the eye could see, giving reassuring confirmation that we were indeed far north of Seoul, touching the mountainous heart of Gangwon.

The riverbed is smartly zoned into specialized sectors catering to every kind of visitor: general fishing zones, dedicated family zones, children's play parks, and a specialized Foreign Tourist Exclusive Fishing Zone. Thousands of people crouched over circular ice holes, gently bobbing their colorful plastic rods up and down, peering intently into the crystal-clear turquoise waters beneath. Sancheoneo (Korean mountain trout), known to survive only in the purest grade-1 fresh mountain currents, swam playfully beneath the ice sheet.

▲ Global Joy on Ice: An enthusiastic traveler from Hong Kong proudly holding up his fresh catch / Photo by Paeton

Tourists from Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia, and across Southeast Asia flocked in droves to revel in this sub-zero fairy tale. For many hailing from tropical climes, walking atop a frozen river was an unimaginable novelty. Nearby, a traveler from Taiwan hooked a plump, glistening Sancheoneo and erupted in joyous, childlike screams of “Great! Great!” that echoed across the entire valley, bringing warm smiles to all who watched.

3. The Brave & The Warm: Bare-Hand Catching & Steaming Foot Baths

Beyond the patient serenity of ice fishing lies the undisputed adrenaline peak of the festival: the Bare-Hand Sancheoneo Catching Competition. At the entrance of the arena, a towering ice sculpture sculpted in intricate detail heralded the Year of the Golden Pig (Gihae Year), glistening under the winter sunlight. Here, courageous participants donned nothing but festival T-shirts and shorts, taking a deep breath before diving bare-legged into ice-cold mountain pools to scoop up darting trout with their bare hands!

▲ The Thrill of Sub-Zero Bravery: Snatching a lively mountain trout with bare hands from the frigid pool / Photo by Paeton

Screams of shock turned into roars of triumph as challengers slipped the wriggling fish into their shirts or clenched them firmly in their teeth for celebratory cheers!

Yet what touched our hearts even more deeply was the festival organizers’ meticulous, heartfelt care for human well-being. Directly beside the bare-hand pool, large wooden tubs filled with steaming hot water were prepared: the Warm Foot Bath Facility (족욕탕). Shivering participants immediately sat down side-by-side to submerge their numb feet into the soothing warmth, sharing laughter and thawing away any risk of frostbite. It was a shining testament to Korean thoughtfulness and safety standards.

▲ Melting the Chill Away: Visitors warming their frozen feet in the outdoor steaming foot bath / Photo by Paeton

4. From River to Table: The Culinary Art of Mountain Trout

No festival experience is complete without indulging in the reward of your labor. Freshly caught Sancheoneo can be taken directly to the on-site culinary pavilions, where visitors can savor their prize either as delicate, ruby-hued raw sashimi (회) or as fragrant, piping-hot wood-fired salt grilled trout (산천어구이).

The visual spectacle of the grilling pavilion is unforgettable: dozens of gargantuan, cylindrical roasters filled with glowing oak charcoal. Master grillers carefully salt the fresh fish, wrap them snugly in aluminum foil, and slide them into individual roasting drawers. In just 15 minutes, the fish emerges steaming, its outer skin crisped to perfection while the tender, succulent meat retains all its natural, buttery sweetness.

▲ Winter's Richest Flavor: Salt-grilled mountain trout steaming hot from oak-charcoal roasters / Photo by Paeton

A modest preparation fee (around 2,000 KRW) pays for the cleaning and grilling service. Adding to the delightful local flavor, Chuncheon’s historic Ethiopia Bet Coffee—Korea’s first specialized coffeehouse established in 1968—operated a cozy pop-up mobile stall on the festival grounds, filling the frosty mountain air with the rich aroma of hand-dripped Ethiopian beans.

5. Editorial Reflections: The Secret Behind Hwacheon's Global Stature

Witnessing the Hwacheon Sancheoneo Ice Festival in full swing makes it abundantly clear why foreign journalists and global travelers praise it as one of the best-organized winter festivals on earth. Its true success is not merely measured in tourist headcounts, but in the harmony of community execution:

  • Seamless Civilian-Military-Government Synergy: County officials, local residents, and stationed military personnel work as a single unified team.
  • Unsung Dedication of Elderly Volunteers: Warm-hearted senior citizens acting as greeters, safety guides, and cultural ambassadors, lending the festival its unmistakable warmth.
  • Sophisticated Administrative Care: Well-maintained heating zones, immaculate sanitation, and fair exchange voucher systems that inject economic vitality directly back into local small businesses.

While some environmental critics have raised valid questions regarding ecological welfare and water purity post-festival, Hwacheon County has actively responded with robust riverbed purification programs, water replenishment protocols, and strict monitoring. By addressing these challenges transparently and respectfully, Hwacheon will continue to set the golden standard for sustainable, eco-conscious winter tourism.
