🌿 Asia’s Premier Ecological Treasury: National Institute of Ecology & Ecorium (국립생태원 에코리엄, Seocheon)
🌍 A living biome journey encompassing the Tropical, Desert, Mediterranean, Temperate, and Polar worlds

🏛️ Ecorium Glass Sanctuary: The monumental, eco-sculpted dome embracing the Earth's five vital climate zones in Seocheon / Photo by Paeton

Situated on an expansive 1 million square-meter canvas in Seocheon, Chungcheongnam-do, the National Institute of Ecology (국립생태원) stands as South Korea's most ambitious ecological research and exhibition complex. Crowned by the awe-inspiring architectural marvel known as Ecorium (에코리엄), this world-class bio-center allows visitors to traverse the equator, arid deserts, Mediterranean shores, temperate woodlands, and frozen polar caps—all within a single afternoon.

For foreign travelers seeking more than standard metropolitan sightseeing, this ecological haven reveals the profound harmony between cutting-edge bioclimatic architecture and the living spirit of the planet. Here, over 5,400 species of flora and fauna thrive under computer-controlled microclimates, offering an unforgettable encounter with the wild pulse of nature.

🌳 The Sentinel of Africa: The legendary Baobab tree (Adansonia digitata), evocative of Antoine de Saint-Exupéry’s The Little Prince / Photo by Paeton

1. From Steaming Rainforests to the Arid Splendor of the Baobab

Upon crossing the threshold into the Tropical Biome, warm misty air and dense emerald foliage immediately envelop travelers. Towering ficus trees, climbing lianas, and cascading indoor waterfalls recreate the primordial rhythm of Amazonian and Southeast Asian rainforests.

Transitioning into the Desert Biome reveals a sudden shift into golden sunlight and striking sculptural cacti. Here, the undisputed crown belongs to the mighty African Baobab Tree, whose massive, swollen trunk stores precious water against prolonged droughts. Surrounded by Madagascar spiny thickets, Mojave Joshua trees, and Atacama succulent formations, the desert pavilion presents nature's ultimate resilience against extreme aridity.

2. Amazon River Giants and the Aquatic Realm

Beneath the rainforest canopy, crystal-clear mega-tanks showcase the aquatic wonders of the world's greatest freshwater basins. Foremost among them is the legendary Pirarucu (Arapaima gigas)—a living fossil swimming with prehistoric grace, its armoring scales shimmering with silver and crimson hues.

Vibrant schools of tropical river fish, rare freshwater stingrays, and delicate aquatic flora offer an immersive vantage point into the intricate web of freshwater biodiversity.

3. Serene Reed Trails and Outdoor Wetland Sanctuaries

Stepping outside Ecorium, visitors find themselves enveloped by the tranquil outdoor wetlands of Seocheon. Meandering wooden boardwalks cut through endless expanses of rustling golden reeds and native marshes.

This landscape seamlessly connects with the spirit of the nearby Sinseong-ri Reed Field (신성리 갈대밭), one of Korea's four greatest reed beds where the Geumgang River meets the West Sea. In autumn, when the silver plumes sway against azure skies, the walking trail becomes a contemplative sanctuary where human thoughts melt into the calming rhythm of the marshland breeze.

🌍 Traveler’s Essential Guide to National Institute of Ecology

  • Address: 1210 Geumgang-ro, Maseo-myeon, Seocheon-gun, Chungcheongnam-do, South Korea (충남 서천군 마서면 금강로 1210 국립생태원)
  • Operating Hours: 09:30 – 18:00 (March to October) / 09:30 – 17:00 (November to February). Closed on Mondays.
  • Admission: Adults: ₩5,000 / Teenagers: ₩3,000 / Children: ₩2,000 (Very economical for a world-class exhibition).
  • Transit Access: Directly adjacent to Janghang Station (장항역) on the Janghang Railway Line (Saemaeul/Mugunghwa trains from Yongsan Station, Seoul, approx. 3 hours). The ecological park's rear gate is connected directly to the station exit.
  • Recommended Itinerary: Spend 3 hours exploring Ecorium's five biomes and outdoor wetlands, then take a 15-minute taxi to Sinseong-ri Reed Field, followed by fresh seafood at Maryangpo Port for sunset.
