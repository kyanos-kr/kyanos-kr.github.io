🌿 An Island Within the Mountains: The Mystical Sanctuary of Cheongpyeongsa (청평사, Chuncheon)
🌊 A Scenic Ferry Voyage Across Lake Soyang & The Legend of the Tang Princess at Guseong Waterfall

🚢 The Inland Sea Odyssey: Embarking on a peaceful ferry ride across Lake Soyang toward the secluded harbor of Cheongpyeongsa Valley / Photo by Paeton

Just a seventy-minute express train journey northeast of Seoul brings travelers to Chuncheon, the romantic lakeside capital of Gangwon Province. While famed worldwide for its culinary delights of spicy stir-fried chicken (Dakgalbi) and buckwheat noodles (Makguksu), Chuncheon hides an even more profound cultural treasure nestled high within the granite spires of Mount Obong (오봉산, 779m): the venerable Buddhist sanctuary of Cheongpyeongsa Temple (청평사).

What makes Cheongpyeongsa extraordinary is its rare mode of arrival. Known as an 'island on land', the temple is most gracefully reached by taking a scenic passenger ferry from the colossal Soyanggang Dam across the expansive fjord-like waters of Lake Soyang. Disembarking at Cheongpyeong-ri dock, visitors enter a lush, tranquil gorge where cascading waterfalls, ancient legends, and thousand-year-old wooden pavilions unfold like a classical Joseon landscape scroll.

⛩️ Thousand-Year Zen Architecture: The solemn temple hall framed by autumn-tinted mountain slopes and ancient Korean granite lanterns / Photo by Paeton

1. The Spiritual Haven of Scholar-Hermit Lee Ja-hyeon

Founded in 973 AD during the reign of King Gwangjong of the Goryeo Dynasty, the temple was originally named Baegamsa. However, its renaissance arrived in 1089 under the prominent high official and philosopher Lee Ja-hyeon (이자현). Renouncing his noble titles and the decadence of the royal court, he retreated into this mountain valley, renaming it Munsuwon and later Cheongpyeongsa (signifying 'Clear, Peaceful Abode').

Lee designed the temple not merely as a monastic center, but as a living philosophical garden. Here stands the famous Hoejeonmun Gate (회전문, National Treasure No. 164), an architectural marvel built in the mid-Joseon period that symbolizes the Buddhist wheel of reincarnation and spiritual awakening. Stepping across its raised wooden threshold, one immediately feels an aura of deep quietude wash over the senses.

🌊 Guseong Waterfall (구송폭포): Nine cascading melodies resonant in crystal mountain waters, tied to the tragic legend of the Tang Princess / Photo by Paeton

2. The Legend of the Tang Princess & Guseong Waterfall

As you hike the 2-kilometer gentle path from the ferry terminal to the temple grounds, nature orchestrates a sensory feast. The jewel of the valley is Guseong Waterfall (Nine Pine Waterfall), plunging nine meters down sheer granite into a turquoise pool. Locals say the tumbling waters create nine distinct musical harmonies depending on your vantage point.

According to lore, an imperial princess of the Chinese Tang Dynasty was cursed with an unwanted suitor who transformed into a giant snake, clinging to her body. Seeking salvation across the seas, she wandered to Cheongpyeongsa Valley. After bathing in the crystal waters and taking refuge inside the temple's cave, the snake was struck down by heavenly thunder, freeing the princess. In deep gratitude, she funded the construction of the three-story stone pagoda that still overlooks the cliff today.

3. Essential Practical Tips for International Travelers

  • How to Get There: Take the ITX-Cheongchun train from Yongsan or Cheongnyangni Station to Chuncheon Station (approx. 70 mins). From Chuncheon Station Exit 1, board Local Bus 11 or 12 to Soyang Dam Ferry Terminal (30 mins).
  • Ferry Schedule: Boats operate every 30 minutes from Soyang Dam to Cheongpyeongsa Dock (round-trip fare approx. 10,000 KRW). The scenic boat journey takes roughly 15 minutes.
  • Walking & Hiking: The stroll from the boat dock to Cheongpyeongsa is a gentle 35-minute walk along a clear valley brook, suitable for all fitness levels. For avid trekkers, climbing behind the temple to the summits of Obongsan offers jaw-dropping panoramic views of Lake Soyang.
  • Gastronomic Pairing: Traditional riverside restaurants near the boat dock serve authentic acorn jelly (Dotorimuk), potato pancakes (Gamjajeon), and chilled Makgeolli rice wine brewed in Gangwon.
