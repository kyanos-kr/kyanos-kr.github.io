💧 An Inbound Discovery in Northern Gyeonggi-do: Lake Sanjeongho (산정호수) & Mount Myeongseongsan (명성산) in Pocheon City
🍁 Walking along the floating waterside trails, listening to the silver-grass murmurs of King Gungye's ancient lamentations

🏞️ Mountain Oasis of Pocheon: Lake Sanjeongho framed by the dramatic granite ridge of Myeongseongsan / Photo by Paeton

The northern territories of Gyeonggi Province bear a unique geographical character—a harmonious blend of resilient mountainous backbones and serene man-made reservoirs carved out during the mid-20th century. Among these, none holds greater aesthetic renown than Lake Sanjeongho (산정호수), literally translating to "a lake tucked deeply into a well-like mountain bowl."

Nestled between Mount Myeongseongsan and Mount Mangbongsan in Pocheon City, this tranquil body of water covers nearly 24 hectares. Originally constructed in 1925 as an agricultural reservoir for downstream rice paddies, it has evolved over decades into one of Korea’s premier eco-tourism retreats, where dense seasonal colors and quiet waters reflect the shifting seasons.

🚶‍♂️ Floating Promenade: The 4-kilometer lakeside wooden boardwalk offering meditative steps over the glassy surface / Photo by Paeton

1. A Meditative 4km Circuit: Strolling Upon the Water's Surface

Arriving at the entrance from the Uncheon Bus Terminal via local transit, visitors first encounter the cheerful buzz of traditional eateries and family leisure facilities. Yet, venturing just past the lively entrance plaza onto the eastern rim reveals an immediate transformation into pure woodland serenity.

A continuous 4-kilometer walking trail completely encircles the lake. Broad sections are built as floating wooden decks directly atop the water, giving travelers the ethereal sensation of walking upon ripples. Dense pine forests canopy the banks, releasing crisp phytoncides that revitalize the senses. Walking here in late autumn, with crimson maples and golden oaks blanketing the moist earth, offers an unforgettable pause from urban hustle.

2. Mount Myeongseongsan: The Mountain of Weeping and Silver Grass

Directly embracing the northeastern flank of the lake stands Mount Myeongseongsan (명성산, 922.6m), famously known as the "Crying Mountain." According to poignant historical lore from the Later Three Kingdoms period, King Gungye (궁예) of Taebong fled into these rugged ravines after being betrayed by his generals in 918 AD. His tragic lamentations echoed so fiercely against the towering rock walls that the mountain itself seemed to cry in sympathy.

Today, the mountain is widely celebrated as one of Korea’s top five silver-grass (Eulalia) colonies. Every October, the vast plateau near Samgakbong peak turns into an undulating ocean of shimmering silver plumes, swaying gently under golden autumn sunlight. At the foot of the hiking trail, Biseon Falls (비선폭포) and Deungryong Falls plunge through dramatic granite ravines, offering cool mists and invigorating vitality.

3. Essential Inbound Guide & Transportation Tips

📍 Practical Travel Information

  • Location: Sanjeongho-ro 411beon-gil, Yeongbuk-myeon, Pocheon-si, Gyeonggi-do (경기도 포천시 영북면 산정호수로 411번길)
  • Recommended Season: Mid-October to Mid-November (Autumn foliage & Silver Grass Festival) or Spring (May fresh greenery).
  • Walking Course: Waterside Wooden Boardwalk Circuit (~4.0 km, approximately 1 hour 20 minutes at a relaxed pace).
  • Mountain Hiking: Lake Trailhead $\rightarrow$ Biseon Falls $\rightarrow$ Deungryong Falls $\rightarrow$ Silver Grass Plateau (approx. 2.5 hours round trip).
  • Nearby Highlights: Jainsa Buddhist Temple, Herb Island, Hantangang River Geopark, Bidulginang Cascade, Pocheon Traditional Hangwa Museum.
  • Public Transit from Seoul:
    • From Dong Seoul Bus Terminal (Gangbyeon Station, Line 2): Take intercity express bus to Uncheon Terminal (운천터미널, approx. 1h 20m), then board local bus No. 138-6 or 70 to Sanjeongho (15 mins).
    • From Seoul Yangjae / Gangnam Station: Take Express Metrolink Bus No. 3100 directly to Pocheon and transfer to local feeder routes.

Standing along the suspension footbridge spanning the lake’s gentle narrows, watching the early morning mist rise softly from the reservoir into the surrounding pine boughs, one realizes why Pocheon remains a cherished retreat for poets and weary souls alike. It is a sanctuary where deep history and unspoiled natural rhythms beat as one.
