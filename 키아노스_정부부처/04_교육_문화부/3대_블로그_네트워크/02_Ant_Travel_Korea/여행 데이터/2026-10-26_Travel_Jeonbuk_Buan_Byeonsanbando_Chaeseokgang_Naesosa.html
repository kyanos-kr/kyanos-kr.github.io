🌊 Korea's Sole Peninsula National Park: Byeonsanbando (부안 변산반도 국립공원) in Buan-gun, Jeollabuk-do
🚶‍♂️ An Exclusive Inbound Exploration: Chaeseokgang Stratified Cliffs, Mystical Sea Caves, Gyeokpohang Fresh Seafood & Thousand-Year Naesosa Fir Canopy

📚 Nature's Geological Library: The prehistoric stratified sedimentary cliff beds of Chaeseokgang standing tall against West Sea swells / Photo by Paeton

Among South Korea’s 23 designated National Parks, Byeonsanbando National Park (변산반도 국립공원) occupies an utterly unmatched ecological position. Designated in 1988, it is the country’s only peninsula-type national park, harmoniously divided into two distinct realms: Naebyeonsan (Inner Byeonsan) with its rugged mountain valleys and peaceful mountain hermitages, and Oebyeonsan (Outer Byeonsan) boasting dramatic wave-cut sea cliffs, golden tidal mudflats, and lively fishing harbors.

Located along the southwest coast in Buan County, Jeollabuk-do, Byeonsanbando feels like a living historical and geological scroll. Here, millions of years of Cretaceous lake sedimentation have been exposed by relentless marine tides, creating rock formations resembling tens of thousands of ancient encyclopedias stacked neatly on the ocean's edge.

1. Chaeseokgang: A Geological Miracle Carved Over 70 Million Years

The crown jewel of Outer Byeonsan is unmistakably Chaeseokgang (채석강), designated as Natural Monument No. 28. Named after the famed Chinese river where legendary poet Li Bai (이태백) reportedly fell into the water while trying to embrace the moon’s shimmering reflection, Chaeseokgang commands breathtaking natural majesty.

At low tide, the ocean graciously retreats to reveal vast wave-cut rock platforms and mysterious sea caves. Walking directly upon the exposed rocky sea floor, international visitors are enchanted by the sheer, rhythmic regularity of the sedimentary strata—alternating layers of shale, sandstone, and conglomerate compressed together under titanic prehistoric pressures.

2. Naesosa Temple: Awakening Inner Peace Along the Fir Tree Canopy

Nestled deep within Naebyeonsan’s tranquil mountain embrace lies Naesosa Temple (내소사), originally founded in 633 AD during the Baekje Kingdom. Its very name, Naeso (來蘇), carries a profound, comforting meaning: "May all who come here be reborn into tranquility and joy."

From the Iljumun (One Pillar Gate) to the temple courtyard, visitors walk through an iconic 600-meter avenue flanked by majestic, straight fir trees that are over 150 years old. Recognized as one of Korea's top forest pathways, this aromatic walk cleanses mental fatigue before you arrive at the Main Hall (Daeungbojeon, Treasure No. 291), famous worldwide for its intricate, unpainted wooden floral lattice doors crafted without a single iron nail.

3. Gyeokpohang Harbor & Fresh Ocean Gastronomy

Adjacent to Chaeseokgang lies Gyeokpohang Port (격포항), a Class-1 fishing harbor renowned as a vital seafood logistics hub of the West Sea. After hiking the coastal boardwalk and rock ledges, travelers gather at the vibrant fish market to relish Buan’s signature culinary treasures: sweet short-neck clam porridge (baekhapjuk), fresh blue crabs, seasoned stingray, and rich grilled rockfish.

🗺️ Inbound Traveler's Essential Field Guide: Byeonsanbando

  • Recommended Route: Morning walk at Naesosa Fir Forest (Inner Byeonsan) → Lunch at Gyeokpohang Port (Fresh Clam Porridge & Seafood) → Afternoon low-tide exploration of Chaeseokgang sedimentary cliffs & sea caves → Sunset at Jeokbyeokgang or Mohang Beach.
  • Crucial Tidal Notice: You can only walk safely on the rocky wave platform and visit the Chaeseokgang sea caves during low tide (간조). Please verify daily tide timetables via the Korea Hydrographic and Oceanographic Agency (KHOA) or local information centers before venturing onto the rocks.
  • Getting There: Express buses run from Seoul Central City Terminal to Buan Bus Terminal (approx. 2 hrs 40 mins). From Buan Terminal, take local bus routes toward Gyeokpo / Naesosa (approx. 40-50 mins), or hire a local taxi.
  • Admission & Parking: National Park walking grounds and Chaeseokgang are open year-round with free entry. Dedicated paid parking facilities are available at both Gyeokpohang and Naesosa.
