Located along the peaceful southwestern coastline of Hwaseong City in Gyeonggi Province, Gungpyeonghang Port (궁평항) is one of South Korea's most atmospheric coastal sanctuaries within an easy day trip from Seoul. Historically named 'Gungpyeong'—meaning the fertile coastal plains once managed directly by the Joseon royal palace—this vibrant harbor weaves together working fishing fleets, vast ecological mudflats, century-old pine forests, and traditional Korean architecture into an unforgettable seaside symphony.

1. The 4th Wonder of Hwaseong: The Golden Ritual of 'Gungpyeong Sunset'

The undisputed crown jewel of the harbor is the 'Gungpyeong Sunset' (궁평낙조), officially honored as the fourth scenic treasure among the Eight Wonders of Hwaseong. At the very tip of the long stone seawall stands a graceful traditional Korean pavilion. In the bright afternoon sun, the Yellow Sea sparkles like millions of silver coins scattered across a royal promenade as fishing boats cut briskly through the emerald waves.

As dusk descends, the fiery sun melts slowly into the western horizon, painting the curved eaves of the pavilion, the moored fishing vessels, and the wet mudflats in breathtaking hues of crimson, amber, and deep violet.

2. Walking Above the Waves: The Overwater Fishing Pier & Living Mudflats

Stretching nearly 200 meters directly out over the water is the famous Gungpyeong Fishing Pier—an expansive wooden boardwalk bridge supported by marine pillars. At high tide, travelers experience the liberating thrill of walking right above the rolling waves while local anglers cast their lines along the seawall.

When the tide retreats, the harbor unveils its quiet sanctuary: an immense, breathing West Sea tidal flat (Getbeol). Seagulls and migratory shorebirds step gently across the shallow ripples, celebrating a timeless ecological ritual.

3. Coastal Totem Poles, Century-Old Pines & The Seafood Market

Along the coastal cliffs beside the harbor, traditional carved wooden totem poles (Jangseung) greet visitors with warm, rustic smiles, standing as folk guardians praying for safe voyages and abundant catches. Just beyond lies Gungpyeong-ri Beach, where more than a thousand century-old black pine trees form a lush seaside forest.

To complete the journey, travelers gather at the expansive Gungpyeonghang Seafood Market. Filled with lively stalls offering freshly caught flounder, blue crabs, jumbo prawns, and sweet manila clams, it is the ultimate place to savor a hot bowl of handmade clam noodle soup (Bajirak Kalguksu) after a sunset walk.

📍 International Traveler's Pocket Guide

  • Location: 1049-24 Gungpyeonghang-ro, Seosin-myeon, Hwaseong-si, Gyeonggi-do, South Korea (경기도 화성시 서신면 궁평항로 1049-24)
  • Public Transit from Suwon/Seoul: Take Subway Line 1 to Suwon Station, then board Bus No. 400 or 400-4 directly to the final stop at Gungpyeonghang Port (~1 hr 15 mins).
  • Admission & Parking: Free admission year-round; large free public parking lot available.
  • Recommended Walking Course: Breakwater & Sunset Pavilion ➔ Overwater Fishing Pier ➔ Coastal Totem Pole Cliff Trail & Pine Forest ➔ Gungpyeonghang Seafood Market (approx. 2 hours).
  • Local Flavors: Freshly sliced flounder & rockfish sashimi, grilled jumbo prawns (autumn specialty), and steaming Bajirak Kalguksu (clam knife-cut noodles).
