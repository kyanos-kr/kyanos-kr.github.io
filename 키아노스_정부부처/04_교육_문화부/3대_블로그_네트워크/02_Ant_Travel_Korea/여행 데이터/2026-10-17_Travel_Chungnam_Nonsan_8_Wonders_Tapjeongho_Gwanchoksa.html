Nestled in the heart of Chungcheongnam-do, Nonsan (논산) is celebrated across South Korea not only as a fertile basin of legendary sweetness and rich agriculture, but as a deeply historic sanctuary that witnessed the climactic final chapters of the ancient Kingdom of Baekje. From the vast, reflective mirror of Tapjeongho Lake (탑정호) to the monumental thousand-year-old stone Buddha at Gwanchoksa Temple (관촉사) and the solemn battlefields of General Gyebaek’s Hwangsanbeol (황산벌), Nonsan offers international travelers an extraordinary journey weaving tranquil nature, spiritual heritage, and heroic epic poetry.

1. Tapjeongho Lake: A Mirror of Serenity and Floating Boardwalks

As the second largest reservoir in Chungcheongnam-do, Tapjeongho Lake spans across multiple valleys, embraced by gently rolling foothills. Originally constructed in 1944 to irrigate the sprawling Nonsan plain, the lake has transformed into an eco-cultural haven celebrated for its crystalline waters and breathtaking evening sunsets.

Along its scenic perimeter lies the Tapjeongho Waterfront Sobaekgil Trail (둘레길)—a scenic wooden deck boardwalk suspended directly above the water. Walking along this path feels like gliding across a liquid mirror. Gentle lake breezes carry the scent of sweet reeds and pine needles, while migratory aquatic birds dip silently into the tranquil surface.

2. Gwanchoksa Temple & the Eunjin Mireuk: A Millennium-Old Wonder

Rising gracefully along the slopes of Mt. Banyasan stands Gwanchoksa Temple (관촉사), designated as the First Wonder among the 8 Scenic Wonders of Nonsan. Founded in 968 AD during the early Goryeo Dynasty by the eminent monk Hyejo, the temple is world-renowned for housing Korea’s largest standing stone Buddha statue: the Stone Standing Maitreya Bodhisattva of Gwanchoksa, affectionately known as 'Eunjin Mireuk' (은진미륵, National Treasure No. 323).

Towering over 18.12 meters in height, this colossal monolithic carving took 38 arduous years to sculpt from massive granite blocks. Unlike the delicate Hellenistic proportions of classic Silla art, the Eunjin Mireuk exudes a monumental, folk-inspired mystique—with an oversized head, serene almond eyes, an elaborate ceremonial crown (bochegwan), and graceful lotus gestures that have consoled the common people for over a thousand years.

3. General Gyebaek’s Memorial & Hwangsanbeol: The Last Stand of Baekje

Just a stone’s throw from Tapjeongho Lake stands the solemn Historic Site of General Gyebaek (계백장군유적지) and the Baekje Military Museum. In the year 660 AD, facing a massive combined force of 50,000 Silla troops and Tang allies, Baekje’s greatest general, Gyebaek, led a suicide vanguard of only 5,000 loyal warriors to the plains of Hwangsanbeol.

Determined to save his family from the humiliation of enslavement, Gyebaek bade farewell to his household with his own sword before marching into battle. Against overwhelming odds of ten to one, his 5,000 warriors repelled the elite Silla army four consecutive times before finally falling in heroic defeat. The solemn monument and museum grounds offer international visitors a deeply moving window into the chivalric ethos and patriotic sacrifice that define Korean historical memory.

4. Essential Inbound Traveler’s Field Guide to Nonsan

📍 Key Destinations & Addresses

  • Tapjeongho Lake & Suspension Bridge: 111 Tapjeong-ro, Bujeok-myeon, Nonsan-si, Chungcheongnam-do (충남 논산시 부적면 탑정로 111)
  • Gwanchoksa Temple (Eunjin Mireuk): 1 Gwanchok-ro 1-gil, Nonsan-si, Chungcheongnam-do (충남 논산시 관촉로1길 1)
  • General Gyebaek Memorial & Baekje Military Museum: 311 Chunggok-ro, Bujeok-myeon, Nonsan-si, Chungcheongnam-do (충남 논산시 부적면 충곡로 311)

🚆 Getting There from Seoul

  • By KTX / ITX-Saemaeul: Take the KTX from Yongsan Station to Nonsan Station (approx. 1 hour 15 minutes). From Nonsan Station, local buses (Nos. 202, 204, 214) or a short 15-minute taxi ride reach Gwanchoksa Temple and Tapjeongho Lake.
  • By Express Bus: From Seoul Central City Terminal (Honam Line) to Nonsan Express Bus Terminal (approx. 2 hours 10 minutes).

💡 Ant’s Pro Travel Tips

  • Best Sunset Timing: Visit Tapjeongho Lake in late afternoon (around 5:00 PM to 6:30 PM in autumn/spring) to capture the crimson sunset reflecting off the suspension bridge and water.
  • Local Gastronomy: Don’t miss Nonsan’s world-famous Strawberries (especially during the winter and spring harvest season) and freshwater spicy catfish stew (Mauntang) along the lake restaurants.
  • Admission: Gwanchoksa Temple and the Baekje Military Museum grounds offer free or very nominal entry, making this an exceptionally high-value cultural heritage day trip.
