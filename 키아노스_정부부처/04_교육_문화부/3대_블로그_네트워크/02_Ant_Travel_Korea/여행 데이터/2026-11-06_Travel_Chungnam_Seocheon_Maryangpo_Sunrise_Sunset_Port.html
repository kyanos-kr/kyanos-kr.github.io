🌅 A Geographical Miracle of the Korean Peninsula: Maryangpo Port (마량포구, Seocheon)
🌊 Witnessing Both Sunrise and Sunset Over the Shimmering Waters of Biin Bay

🌅 The Golden Pillar of Dawn: The morning sun rising over the tranquil horizon of Maryangpo Port on the West Sea / Photo by Paeton

To most travelers, the geography of the Korean Peninsula follows a simple rule: one journeys to the East Sea (동해) to greet the rising sun and visits the West Sea (서해, Yellow Sea) to bid farewell to the evening dusk. Yet along the southern coast of South Chungcheong Province lies an extraordinary exception—Maryangpo Port (마량포구) in Seocheon County, where nature performs the rare miracle of unveiling both a majestic ocean sunrise and a fiery coastal sunset from the very same shoreline.

Because the Maryang-ri peninsula juts out into the West Sea and curves gracefully toward the southeast, embracing the calm waters of Biin Bay (비인만), travelers standing on its breakwater between late autumn and early spring can watch the sun emerge directly from the sea at dawn and sink back into the western horizon at twilight.

⚓ Poetic Silence at Daybreak: A fishing vessel gliding past the lighthouse as the amber light of dawn awakens the harbor / Photo by Paeton

1. The Symphony of Dawn and Dusk: A Contemplative Coastal Sanctuary

Arriving at Maryangpo before daybreak offers an experience of profound stillness. As the dark velvet sky softens into shades of amber and rose, small fishing boats set out quietly past the white lighthouse at the tip of the breakwater. Unlike the crashing surf of the eastern coastline, the waters of Maryangpo ripple with gentle serenity, turning the rising sun's reflection into a shimmering path of liquid gold.

When evening returns, the port transforms once again. As the crimson sun descends toward the islands on the horizon, it bathes the weathered hands of local fishermen and the quiet tidal flats in a warm, nostalgic glow—inviting travelers to pause, reflect on the passage of time, and renew their hopes for tomorrow.

🌊 Shimmering Tidal Flats of Biin Bay: Silvery sunlight dancing across the UNESCO-recognized coastal mudflats and distant islands / Photo by Paeton

2. Living History, Ecological Richness, and Seasonal Maritime Festivals

Beyond its celestial spectacles, Maryangpo holds a unique place in Korean cultural history. In 1816, British naval vessels Alceste and Lyra anchored off Maryangjin, marking the historic moment when the first English-language Bible was transmitted to the Korean shore—commemorated today at the nearby Korea First Bible Transmission Monument Park.

The surrounding tidal flats (Getbol) teem with marine life, fueling renowned local culinary festivals throughout the year: the vibrant Webfoot Octopus (Jukkumi) and Camellia Festival in spring, the prized Gizzard Shad (Jeoneo) Festival in autumn, and the heartwarming Sunrise & Sunset Festival every December 31st and January 1st.

3. Essential Practical Guide for International Travelers

  • Location & Address: 339-4 Maryang-ri, Seo-myeon, Seocheon-gun, Chungcheongnam-do (충남 서천군 서면 마량리 339-4).
  • Best Season for Sunrise & Sunset: From November through February (especially around the Winter Solstice, Dongji), the solar angle aligns perfectly across Biin Bay, offering the most dramatic ocean sunrise views on the West Coast.
  • How to Get There: Take the Korail Mugunghwa or Saemaeul train from Yongsan Station (Seoul) to Seocheon Station (서천역) (approx. 2 hours 30 mins), then board a local bus or taxi to Maryangpo Port (approx. 25–30 mins).
  • Nearby Attractions to Combine: Pair your harbor visit with the 500-year-old Maryang-ri Camellia Forest & Dongbaekjeong Pavilion (just 5 minutes away) and the world-class National Institute of Ecology (Ecorium) in Seocheon.
