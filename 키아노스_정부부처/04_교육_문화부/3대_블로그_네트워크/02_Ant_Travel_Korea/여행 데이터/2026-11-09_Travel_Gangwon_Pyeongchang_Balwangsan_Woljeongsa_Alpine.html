Situated high upon the dramatic eastern spine of the Korean Peninsula, Pyeongchang (평창) is widely celebrated across the globe as the cradle of the 2018 Winter Olympic Games—where human grit and sporting nobility shone against snow-capped peaks. Yet beyond its world-class winter sporting complexes lies an even deeper, ancient harmony: pristine alpine wilderness, spiritual Buddhist sanctuaries dating back over thirteen centuries, and cool mountain breezes that cleanse the senses in every season.

For international travelers yearning to explore Korea beyond bustling metropolitan centers, Pyeongchang offers an unparalleled alpine odyssey. Here, one can glide seamlessly over deep green forested valleys on Korea’s longest cable car to touch the clouds at Mount Balwangsan (발왕산), wander beneath the fragrant evergreen canopies of Woljeongsa Temple’s thousand-year fir forest, and trace the heroic legacy of the Olympic champions at Alpensia.

🚡 Ascending into the Clouds: The Balwangsan Cable Car journeying across a 3.7-kilometer scenic expanse over verdant ridges / Photo by Paeton

1. Mount Balwangsan: Soaring Above the Clouds on the Skywalk

Mount Balwangsan, soaring to 1,458 meters, ranks as the twelfth highest mountain in South Korea. The journey begins with the Balwangsan Cable Car at Mona Yongpyong Resort, spanning 3.7 kilometers (7.4 km round-trip) and taking visitors on a tranquil 20-minute aerial cruise across rolling alpine ridges and pristine ancient valleys.

At the summit station stands the iconic Balwangsan Ki Skywalk (기스카이워크). Extending boldly into the open sky with transparent reinforced glass floors, it grants adventurous travelers the breathtaking feeling of walking on air above endless waves of forested peaks. Nearby, ancient yew trees (Jumok)—renowned in Korean folklore for living a thousand years and standing preserved for another thousand—stand as silent witnesses to time, alongside the pure mineral spring of Balwangsu.

🌲 Footsteps of Mindfulness: Walking barefoot along the sacred 1,000-year fir tree forest path leading to Woljeongsa Temple / Photo by Paeton

2. Woljeongsa Temple & Mount Odaesan: The Thousand-Year Zen Sanctuary

Descending from Balwangsan into the gentle folds of Odaesan National Park, travelers encounter one of Korea's most venerated spiritual sanctuaries: Woljeongsa Temple (월정사). Founded in 643 AD during the Silla Dynasty by the great Master Jajang, Woljeongsa is famed for its serene 1-kilometer entrance path flanked by over 1,700 ancient needle fir trees (Jeonnamu).

Walking along this flat, earthen pathway—often undertaken barefoot as a form of meditative forest bathing (Shinrin-yoku)—fills the lungs with crisp, phytoncide-rich mountain air. The sound of clear crystal waters flowing through Geumgangyeon Ravine mingles with the distant temple bell, guiding pilgrims past the Iljumun Gate toward the central courtyard where the magnificent National Treasure Octagonal Nine-Story Stone Pagoda stands in timeless grace.

🛕 Ancient Buddhist Grace: The timeless courtyard and prayer halls of Woljeongsa Temple, founded in 643 AD / Photo by Paeton

3. Alpensia Olympic Legacy & Highland Gastronomy

No visit to Pyeongchang is complete without experiencing its living Olympic heritage at Alpensia Resort. The towering Olympic Ski Jumping Stadium, where competitors once soared against the backdrop of snowy Gangwon ridges, remains open year-round for visitors to tour the panoramic observation deck, ski museum, and championship biathlon courses.

After a day of high-altitude exploration, Pyeongchang’s culinary treasures offer wholesome rejuvenation. Treat yourself to a steaming bowl of Mountain Wild Herb Bibimbap (산채비빔밥) seasoned with perilla oil and herbs freshly foraged from Mount Odaesan, or savor traditional Buckwheat Noodles (Memil Makguksu) and delicate buckwheat pancakes in nearby Bongpyeong—a region immortalized by Korean modern literature.

4. Essential Practical Guide for International Travelers

  • Geographic Location: Pyeongchang County, Gangwon State (강원특별자치도 평창군).
  • How to Reach from Seoul: Board the high-speed KTX-Eum train from Seoul Station or Cheongnyangni Station to Jinbu Station (진부역, Odaesan). Travel time is just 1 hour and 20 minutes. Regular shuttle buses and taxis connect Jinbu Station directly to Woljeongsa (15 mins) and Mona Yongpyong / Alpensia (20 mins).
  • Best Seasons to Visit:
    • Autumn (October – November): Fiery crimson maples and golden larches engulf Mount Odaesan and Mount Balwangsan.
    • Winter (December – March): Korea’s premier snow paradise for skiing, snowboarding, and the Daegwallyeong Snow Festival.
    • Spring & Summer: Crisp 700-meter highland breezes offer a refreshing sanctuary from humid summer city heat.
  • Recommended Itinerary: Arrive at Jinbu Station in the morning → Take a meditative stroll along the Woljeongsa Fir Forest Trail → Enjoy lunch at the Odaesan Wild Herb Village → Ride the Balwangsan Cable Car and conquer the Skywalk → Tour the Alpensia Olympic Ski Jumping Tower before returning by KTX.
