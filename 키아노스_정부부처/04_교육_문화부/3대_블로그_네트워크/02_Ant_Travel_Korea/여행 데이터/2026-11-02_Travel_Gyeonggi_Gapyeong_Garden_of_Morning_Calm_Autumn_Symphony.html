🌸 The Crown Jewel of Korean Horticultural Art: The Garden of Morning Calm (아침고요수목원, Gapyeong)
🍁 Autumn Chrysanthemum & Foliage Splendor embracing over 5,000 species of native flora

🌿 The Majestic Tapestry of Morning Calm: A sweeping view across the sunken garden embracing over 5,000 species of flora set against Chukryeongsan Mountain / Photo by Paeton

Nestled deep within the serene embrace of Mount Chukryeong in Gapyeong, Gyeonggi-do, The Garden of Morning Calm (아침고요수목원) stands as South Korea's premier horticultural masterpiece. Established in 1996 with the vision of expressing the profound spiritual beauty of Korea through botanical design, this 330,000-square-meter arboretum eschews rigid Western geometric symmetry in favor of flowing natural curves, organic stone embankments, and seamless integration with the surrounding mountain skyline.

For international travelers venturing beyond Seoul, early November offers an extraordinary spectacle. As crisp morning mist rises from the valleys, fiery red maples, golden ginkgoes, and thousands of fragrant autumn chrysanthemums carpet the sunken gardens in a breathtaking mosaic of vibrant colors, creating an unforgettable encounter with the true soul of Korean nature.

🏮 Seohwayeon Pond & Traditional Hanok Pavilion: The timeless grace of classical Korean landscape architecture where graceful pavilions mirror across tranquil waters / Photo by Paeton

1. Seohwayeon and the Philosophy of the Korean Garden

The emotional epicenter of the arboretum is undoubtedly Seohwayeon (서화연), a traditional Korean pond garden designed after the classical private estates of Joseon scholars. At the water's edge sits a refined wooden pavilion, resting above mirrored reflections of iris, lotus pads, and weeping willows.

Unlike traditional Japanese dry gardens or Versailles-style French formality, Korean gardening philosophy honors nature as the ultimate master artist. The pavilion does not dominate the landscape; rather, it frames the distant peaks, inviting the mountain breeze, chirping birds, and rustling reeds directly into the viewer's contemplative gaze.

2. The Sunken Garden: The Korean Peninsula in Floral Relief

Standing on the high observation deck overlooking the Sunken Garden (하경정원) reveals a profound hidden symbolic design. Viewed from above, the sweeping floral beds, stone pathways, and undulating terraces are sculpted to trace the geographic outline of the Korean Peninsula.

Each bed is planted with a dense rotation of perennials, dwarf conifers, and seasonal blossoms. During autumn, this garden becomes a visual crescendo where scarlet salvia, pastel chrysanthemums, and silver pampas grass ripple in rhythmic waves under the golden afternoon sun.

3. Forest Canopy Trails and Ancient Nut Pine Groves

Beyond the manicured theme gardens lies the wilder heart of the arboretum—the extensive coniferous forest trails. Gapyeong is historically renowned for producing Korea's finest pine nuts, and the towering Korean Nut Pines (잣나무) lining Mount Chukryeong emit high concentrations of revitalizing phytoncides.

Strolling along the Millennium Hill and Forest Bathing Path, the fragrance of evergreen resin mingles with the earthy sweetness of fallen oak leaves. It offers visitors a deeply therapeutic forest-bathing experience (Shinrin-yoku), soothing the senses and restoring profound peace after the energetic bustle of Seoul.

🌍 Traveler’s Essential Guide to The Garden of Morning Calm

  • Address: 432 Sumokwon-ro, Sang-myeon, Gapyeong-gun, Gyeonggi-do, South Korea (경기 가평군 상면 수목원로 432 아침고요수목원)
  • Operating Hours: 08:30 – 19:00 (Open year-round, admission closes 1 hour before sunset).
  • Admission: Adults: ₩11,000 / Youths (Middle & High School): ₩8,500 / Children: ₩7,500.
  • Transit Access: From Seoul, take the ITX-Cheongchun train or Gyeongchun Line subway to Cheongpyeong Station (청평역). From Cheongpyeong Terminal/Station, board the Gapyeong City Tour Bus (Course A) or local Bus No. 30-6 or 30-7 (approx. 25 minutes directly to the main entrance).
  • Seasonal Highlights:
    • Late Autumn (Oct – Nov): Chrysanthemum Exhibition & Peak Chukryeongsan Autumn Foliage.
    • Winter (Dec – Mar): The world-renowned Lighting Festival, illuminating the entire arboretum with millions of eco-friendly LED fairy lights against the snow.
  • Recommended Travel Plan: Combine with a morning visit to Nami Island (남이섬) or the Petite France cultural village, finishing with an afternoon walk and traditional tea ceremony at Seohwayeon pavilion.
