1. The Royal Legend of King Sejo and the Bell-Chiming Cave

Situated in modern Namyangju, just east of Seoul on the rugged shoulder of Mount Ungilsan (610m), Sujongsa Temple (수종사, 水鐘寺) holds a profound place in Korean cultural history. According to the revered 18th-century philosopher Dasan Jeong Yak-yong in his travelogue Yu Sujongsa Gi (遊水鐘寺記), local lore traced its earliest roots back to the ancient Silla kingdom and King Taejo Wang Geon of Goryeo.

However, the definitive renaissance of Sujongsa unfolded in 1458 during the reign of King Sejo of the Joseon Dynasty. Returning from Mount Odaesan after seeking spiritual solace and medicinal waters for his severe skin affliction, the King moored his royal barge overnight near Yangsuri. Deep in the quiet of the night, mysterious, resonant bell chimes echoed from high across the misty peaks of Mount Ungilsan.

Ascending the mountain at dawn, the royal envoys discovered an ancient grotto where natural mineral water drops were trickling down from stalactites onto a rocky ledge with the resonant acoustic timbre of bronze chimes, before an assembly of eighteen stone Arhat statues. Moved by this divine encounter, King Sejo ordered the immediate reconstruction of the sanctuary, bestowing its poetic name Sujongsa—literally “Water Bell Temple”—and personally planted two royal ginkgo saplings to bless the temple’s enduring prosperity.

2. “The Crown Vantage of Eastern Temples” and Joseon Literary Brilliance

Renowned scholar-statesman Seo Geo-jeong (서거정, 1420–1488) immortalized Sujongsa by declaring it “Dongbang Sachal Jung Jeil-ui Jeonmang”—the finest scenic vantage among all temples in the Eastern Kingdom. Looking down from the temple balustrade, the panoramic convergence of the Bukhangang (North Han) and Namhangang (South Han) Rivers at Dumulmeori (두물머리) stretches into infinity beneath rolling mountain silhouettes.

“As autumn arrives, the scenery stirs quiet melancholy;
The night-long rain carries into dawn, swelling the river against the banks.
In the earthly realm below, there is nowhere to kindle hearth-smoke,
Yet this celestial mountain pavilion stands level with the heavens.
Dense white clouds gather—to whom shall I offer them?
Yellow leaves flutter on the wind, the path ahead vast and distant.
I ascend to the Eastern Hall to converse in Zen stillness;
Let no strange night bird cry beneath the radiant moon.”
— Seo Geo-jeong, Poem on Sujongsa Temple

3. Samjeongheon Zen Tea House & Sacred Cultural Treasures

Beyond its majestic geographical vistas, Sujongsa holds celebrated status as one of Korea’s spiritual capitals of Dado (다도, Korean Zen Tea Ceremony). Within the temple grounds rests Samjeongheon (삼정헌, 三鼎軒), an intimate mountain tea hall open to all pilgrims and visitors without charge.

Here, travelers sit comfortably on traditional wooden floorboards, brew fragrant green tea leaves using hot kettle water, and sip quietly while gazing through wide picture-frame windows at the river valley below. Eminent figures of Joseon thought, including the Tea Master Ven. Choui (초의선사) and Dasan Jeong Yak-yong, often gathered at this mountain ridge to share tea, philosophical insights, and poetic verses.

🍵 The Sanctuary of Zen Tea: Samjeongheon, where travelers warm their hands and cleanse the mind / Photo by Paeton

The temple also preserves invaluable cultural treasures, foremost among them the Octagonal Five-Story Stone Pagoda (Treasure No. 1808) and the Stupa of Princess Jeongui (정의옹주 부도). In 1939 and 1970, exquisite gilt-bronze Buddha statuettes and reliquaries dating from the 15th through 17th centuries were recovered from inside these pagodas, affirming Sujongsa’s direct royal patronage by the Joseon royal family.

4. Essential Practical Guide for International Travelers

  • Geographic Location: 186 Bukhangang-ro 433beon-gil, Joan-myeon, Namyangju-si, Gyeonggi-do (경기도 남양주시 조안면 북한강로433번길 186).
  • Public Transit from Seoul: Take the Seoul Metropolitan Gyeongui-Jungang Line (경의중앙선) and alight at Ungilsan Station (운길산역, Exit 2).
    • By Taxi / Shuttle: A 10-minute taxi ride brings you directly to the upper mountain parking lot (approx. KRW 7,000–9,000).
    • By Hiking Trail: A tranquil, well-marked hiking ascent of approximately 45–60 minutes directly from Ungilsan Station through lush pine ridges.
  • Admission & Tea Experience: Temple admission is free. The tea ceremony at Samjeongheon is also offered free of charge (donations welcome to support tea preservation).
  • Best Time to Visit:
    • Autumn (October – November): The golden foliage of the 500-year-old ginkgo tree and crisp morning mist over Dumulmeori make this season incomparable.
    • Sunrise / Early Morning: Photographers from around the globe gather here to capture the morning river fog rolling beneath the peaks like a white ocean.
  • Recommended Nearby Connections: Combine your visit with Dasan Heritage Site (다산유적지), Dumulmeori Water Park & Lotus Island (Semiwon, 세미원), and riverside organic Korean dining along Joan-myeon.
