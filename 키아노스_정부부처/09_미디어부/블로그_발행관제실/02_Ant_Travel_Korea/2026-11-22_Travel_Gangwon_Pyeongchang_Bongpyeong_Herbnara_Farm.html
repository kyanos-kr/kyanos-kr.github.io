🌿 The Pioneer of Korean Herbal Agritourism: Herbnara Farm (평창 허브나라농원), Bongpyeong-myeon
🌸 14 Thematic Healing Sanctuaries, Glasshouse Conservatories, Cultural Galleries & Farm-to-Table Gastronomy

🌿 The High Botanical Sanctuary: A peaceful autumn afternoon amidst blooming flower terraces and the European-style conservatory at Herbnara Farm / Photo by Paeton

For discerning international travelers exploring South Korea, the natural sanctuary of Pyeongchang, Gangwon State offers a profound antidote to hyper-connected modern life. While global audiences remember Pyeongchang as the host of the 2018 Winter Olympics, nestled deep along the pristine rapids of Heungjeong Valley (흥정계곡) lies one of the nation’s best-kept eco-cultural treasures: Herbnara Farm (허브나라농원).

Founded in 1993, Herbnara is celebrated as South Korea’s very first commercial tourist farm dedicated entirely to herbs. Situated at the human biological sweet spot of 700 meters above sea level—where atmospheric pressure and alpine breezes optimize human vitality—this 33,000-square-meter (10,000 pyeong) botanical haven harmonizes over 150 varieties of aromatic herbs, native Korean mountain wildflowers, cultural art galleries, and wholesome farm-to-table dining into a seamless tapestry of wellness.

1. The "Hundred-Year Vow": A Tale of Love, Soil, and Courage

The emotional heartbeat of Herbnara Farm lies in the inspiring life journey of its founders, Director Lee Ho-soon (이호순) and Co-Representative Lee Doo-yi (이두이). Decades ago, while still university sweethearts in Seoul, the couple made a solemn pact: "When the sum of our ages reaches one hundred, we shall leave the city, return to the soil, and farm together."

True to their word, in 1993, when Director Lee turned 51 and his wife reached 49 (exactly 100 years combined), they made an extraordinary leap of faith. Lee resigned from his prestigious position as the CEO of a major corporate conglomerate, while his wife—holding bachelor's and master's degrees in agriculture and horticulture from Seoul National University—packed their lives in Seoul and moved to the rugged mountain valley of Bongpyeong.

What greeted them was a desolate stony riverbank covered in barren boulders. For three painstaking years, they cleared rocks by hand, enriched the sub-alpine soil, and carefully nurtured herb seedlings brought from around the globe. Opening its gates in 1996, Herbnara pioneered South Korea’s Sextuple Agritourism Model (6차 산업)—a holistic fusion of organic primary cultivation, secondary artisanal processing, and tertiary experiential hospitality. Their dedication led Herbnara to receive the New Intellectual Award in Agriculture in 2008 and designation as one of the Ministry of Environment’s Top Eco-Tourism Sites in 2009.

2. Exploring 14 Thematic Sanctuaries & Year-Round Glasshouses

Unlike rigid, geometric botanical arboretums, Herbnara Farm is designed as an intimate narrative stroll where nature, literature, and art gently intertwine across more than ten dedicated themed gardens:

  • The Palette Garden: Situated along the farm’s gentle terraced slopes, this iconic garden arranges vibrant blooms into living brushstrokes of color, resembling an artist's wooden palette under open Gangwon skies.
  • The Shakespeare Garden: A literary homage planted with historic English garden herbs mentioned throughout William Shakespeare's immortal plays and sonnets—from fragrant rosemary ("for remembrance") to soothing rue and thyme.
  • The Butterfly Garden: An ecological sanctuary carefully populated with nectar-rich flowering perennials, attracting fluttering native butterflies and bees throughout late spring and summer.
  • The Cottage Garden & Medieval Garden: Featuring rustic European stone walls, wrought-iron gazebos, classical mythological statues, and soothing water fountains.
  • Solbaram Pine Forest & Valley Trail: A pristine boardwalk winding beneath towering Korean red pines along the bubbling waters of Heungjeong Valley, equipped with timber decks where visitors can dip their feet into cool mountain water.
  • Year-Round Glasshouse Conservatory: Even when harsh Gangwon winters blanket the surrounding mountains in heavy snow, this climate-controlled glass sanctuary remains warmly perfumed with flowering chamomile, jasmine, lemon verbena, and lush scented geraniums.

3. Cultural Enrichment: Museums, Galleries & The Starlight Stage

Herbnara is also a respected regional cultural hub, reflecting the family's artistic background (their daughter, an artist, has sculpted many of the park's whimsical signs and creative installations):

  • Fragrance Spring Herb Museum (향기의 샘): An insightful indoor exhibition tracing the millennia-old history of herbs across human civilization, herbal medicine traditions, and the science of essential oil extraction.
  • Turkish Cultural Gallery (한터울): A rare international cultural salon showcasing Anatolian folk artifacts, handmade Turkish rugs, and glazed ceramics, commemorating historical brotherhood and friendship.
  • The Open-Air Starlight Stage (별빛무대): A timber amphitheater embraced by forest canopies, renowned for hosting intimate acoustic forest concerts with legendary Korean musicians like Lee Moon-sae and classical chamber ensembles.

4. Farm-to-Table Gastronomy & Sensory Craft Workshops

No visit to Herbnara is complete without indulging your palate and hands:

  • Birch House Restaurant (자작나무집): Famous for its exquisite Herb Flower Bibimbap (허브꽃비빔밥)—a bowl brimming with freshly harvested organic herbs, edible petals, and artisanal dressings—alongside savory Herb Beef Rice Bowls and braised rosemary chicken sets.
  • The Garden Cafe: Overlooking the tranquil garden terraces, offering steaming pots of freshly brewed lavender, peppermint, and chamomile teas, accompanied by warm herb scones, cookies, and herb-infused soft ice cream.
  • Hands-On Craft Studio: Travelers can blend custom aromatherapy perfumes, cast gentle all-natural herbal soaps, formulate organic lip balms, and craft transparent gel candles infused with pressed flowers to take home as enduring memories.
  • Herbnara Timber Lodges: For an overnight immersion, cozy timber chalets named after healing herbs (Mint, Thyme, Rosemary) offer overnight stays with complimentary garden access and starlit barbecue decks.

6. A Philosopher's Note: The Healing Economy of Time

The world relentlessly urges us to move faster, optimize relentlessly, and live in perpetual digital acceleration. Yet sitting quietly along the crystal waters of Heungjeong Valley under the gentle shade of ancient birches, with the cool alpine breeze carrying hints of wild mint, one realizes that the most precious things in life cannot be manufactured in haste.

Herbnara Farm took thirty years of unwavering patience to blossom from a field of cold stones into this flourishing sanctuary. In witnessing that quiet triumph, the weary soul is gently reminded: to grow deeply and beautifully, one must first learn to breathe with the season.
