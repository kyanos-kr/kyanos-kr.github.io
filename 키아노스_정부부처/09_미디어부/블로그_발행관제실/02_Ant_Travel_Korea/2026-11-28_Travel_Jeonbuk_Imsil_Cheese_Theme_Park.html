🧀 Birthplace of Korean Cheese: Imsil Cheese Theme Park & Imsil Cheese Village (임실치즈마을)
✨ An In-Depth Inbound Culinary & Rural Heritage Guide to Imsil, Jeonbuk, South Korea

🧀 Essence of Pure Milk: Fresh dairy and aged cheese—what Koreans fondly call the “crystal of milk” / Photo by Paeton

Cheese is often described in Korea as the “sari” (pure essence or crystal) of milk. While fresh milk spoils in a matter of days, aging and fermenting it creates cheese—a food that can be kept and savored for years. In many ways, it mirrors Korea’s traditional doenjang (fermented soybean paste) and cheonggukjang. Just as every Korean household carries its own distinct signature flavor in its fermented pastes, artisan cheese develops its own character, aroma, and hue depending on how it is cultured and matured.

Nestled in the gentle hills of Jeonbuk State (North Jeolla Province), Imsil (임실, 任實) literally means “a village where seeds ripen sturdy and full.” True to its name, this tranquil rural county became the undisputed birthplace of Korean cheese.

1. A Belgian Priest, Two Goats, and Korea’s First Cheese (1967)

The story of Imsil cheese began in 1967 with Father Didier t'Serstevens (known in Korea as Ji Jeong-hwan), a Belgian Catholic priest serving at Imsil Cathedral. Deeply moved by the hardships of local farming families, he sought a sustainable way to lift the village out of poverty. Starting modestly with just two goats brought from the West, he began experimenting with goat milk to craft Korea’s very first domestic cheese.

After years of trial and perseverance, Father Ji succeeded in establishing a thriving local dairy industry—and then selflessly handed over the entire enterprise to the villagers without registering a private trademark. Because the name was never trademarked, countless commercial imitators later appeared across the country; today, it is hard to find a pizza in South Korea that does not claim to use “Imsil cheese.” Yet the authentic soul, craft, and heritage of that generous beginning remain rooted right here in Imsil.

2. Hands-On Cheese Crafting & Village Experiences

Visitors to Imsil can do far more than simply taste cheese—they can step directly into the cheesemaking tradition. Across the county, several dedicated parks and community villages welcome travelers for interactive culinary workshops and countryside fun:

  • Imsil Cheese Theme Park (임실치즈테마파크): A sprawling European-style hillside park where guests can craft authentic Imsil N Cheese (www.cheesepark.kr · Tel: +82-63-643-2300).
  • Imsil Cheese Village (임실치즈마을): The heart of local rural tourism, famous for hands-on mozzarella stretching workshops where visitors pull and knead warm curds by hand (cheese.invil.org · Tel: +82-63-643-3700).
  • Imsil Cheese Pizza Village (임실치즈피자마을): Ideal for families and food lovers eager to bake fresh artisan pizzas topped with local cheese (www.cheesecook.co.kr · Tel: +82-63-642-2700).
  • Appenzell Cheese Experience Center (아펜젤치즈체험장): Inspired by Swiss cheesemaking traditions (www.appenzell.co.kr · Tel: +82-63-644-2009).

🍕 Straight from the Oven: Handcrafted pizza brimming with fresh, stretchy Imsil mozzarella / Photo by Paeton

3. Countryside Charm & Culinary Delights

Beyond the dairy workshops, Imsil preserves the unhurried rhythm of Korean farm life. Travelers—especially families with children—can enjoy riding traditional farm cultivators (gyeongungi), taking ox-cart rides, bottle-feeding young calves, and racing down grassy slopes on sleds.

After working up an appetite in the fresh country air, sitting down to a plate of creamy cheese pasta, warm cheese fondue, or wood-fired pizza tastes entirely different when you have seen the care and heritage behind every bite.

4. Inbound Traveler’s Practical Guide to Imsil

📍 Essential Travel Information

  • Theme Park Address: 50 Doin 2-gil, Seongsu-myeon, Imsil-gun, Jeonbuk-do (전북특별자치도 임실군 성수면 도인2길 50)
  • Cheese Village Address: 4-25 Chimyeongjasan-gil, Imsil-eup, Imsil-gun, Jeonbuk-do (전북 임실군 임실읍 치즈마을길)
  • Operating Hours: 09:00 – 18:00 (Closed Mondays at the Theme Park; advance reservation recommended for group workshops)
  • Admission: Theme Park grounds are free to stroll; hands-on cheese & pizza workshops vary by program.
  • Estimated Tour Duration: 2 to 3 hours for workshops and park exploration.

🚆 How to Get There from Seoul

  • By Train: Take the KTX or ITX-Saemaeul from Yongsan Station (용산역) to Jeonju Station (approx. 1 hr 40 mins) or Imsil Station (임실역) via the Jeolla Line. From Imsil Station, a short 10-minute taxi ride brings you directly to the Cheese Theme Park.
  • By Bus: Intercity buses depart from Seoul Nambu Terminal or Central City Terminal to Imsil Terminal (approx. 3 hours).
  • Combine With: Pair your visit with Jeonju Hanok Village (30 minutes north) or Okjeongho Lake (옥정호) for a memorable Jeonbuk countryside itinerary.
